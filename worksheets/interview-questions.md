# Interview Question Bank

*Print this, bring it, and abandon it freely. The best interviews follow the
story, not the list. Questions are grouped so you can open easy and go deep
later. Follow-ups are bolder-branch trees: any question pairs with
"Tell me more about that."*

## Opening (record a spoken intro: who, where, when)

- What's your full name, and who were you named after?
- Where are we sitting right now? Describe the room for the recording.

## Childhood home and neighborhood

- Where were you born, and where did you grow up? What did the house look,
  sound, and smell like?
- Who lived in the house with you? Who lived next door?
- What was your bedroom like? What was on the walls?
- Where did the family eat dinner, and what did Sunday dinners look like?
- What chores did you have? What did you get in trouble for?

## School and growing up

- What school did you go to? What were you good at? What did you hate?
- Who was your best friend, and what did you two do together?
- What did a Saturday look like when you were ten?
- What games, songs, or radio/TV shows do you remember?
- Did the family have a car? Who drove, and where did you all go?

## Work and money

- What was your first job, and what did it pay?
- How did your parents (or grandparents) earn a living? Where exactly?
- Was money tight? How did the family handle it?
- Did anyone in the family own a business? What was a day there like?

## Courtship, marriage, and family life

- How did you meet [spouse/partner]? Tell me about that first meeting.
- What did you think of them at first? When did you know?
- What was the wedding like? Who was there?
- What was a happy day in your marriage look like?
- How did you decide where to live and raise the family?

## Hard times (approach gently, later in the interview)

- What was the hardest year you can remember? How did the family get
  through it?
- Were there losses or illnesses that changed the family?
- Was anyone ever really angry with anyone else? (You don't need details;
  you need the shape of it.)
- Did the family have any experiences of war, migration, or disaster?

## War, migration, and movement

- When did [ancestor] arrive, and do you know why they came?
- What did they leave behind, and what did they bring?
- What did they say about the crossing or the arrival?
- Did they keep a language, holiday, or recipe from "the old country"?

## Characters and stories

- Who in the family was "the character"? What did they do?
- What story gets told at every Thanksgiving? Tell it as fully as you can.
- What was the family joke? The argument that never healed?
- What did [ancestor] smell like? Sound like? What did their hands look like?

## Objects, places, and paper

- Is there anything in the house that came from them? Was it theirs?
- Are there photos, letters, or papers in a closet somewhere?
- Do you know what happened to [photo/album I have]?

## Beliefs, values, traditions

- What holidays mattered most, and how were they celebrated?
- What did the family believe in — formally (church) or informally ("we
  always vote," "we never miss a funeral")?
- What did the family cook for celebrations?

## Closing

- What do you want the next generations to know about this family?
- What do you want me to ask that I haven't?
- Who else should I talk to?

## Mini question sets for specific people

### A sibling or cousin of an ancestor

- When and where were you born, and where does [ancestor] fall in the order?
- What was [ancestor] like at home — versus what others thought of them?
- What made them laugh? What made them angry?
- Do you remember their wedding? Their work? Their voice?

### An in-law (married in)

- How did you first meet the family? How were you welcomed?
- What surprised you most about their family ways?

### The keeper of papers

- What have you saved, and where is it? (Take photos; don't borrow originals.)
- Who saved it before you, and where did it come from?
