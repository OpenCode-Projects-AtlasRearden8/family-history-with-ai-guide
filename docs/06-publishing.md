# Self-Publishing the Finished Family Book

*Your chapters are done, your photos are named, your facts are verified.
Here's how a book you can hand around the kitchen table comes into the
world — without spending much, without signing anything tricky, and without
sending your family files somewhere you can't see them.*

## Step 1 — Assemble the manuscript (local-first)

Keep the entire book in one folder on your computer:

```
book/
  drafts/          # one file per chapter
  images/          # copies of scans, renamed per the Photos chapter
  manuscript.md    # or .docx — the assembled whole
  covers/
  proofs/          # every exported file toward print
```

Order of a family book that reads well:

1. Title page (title, your name as compiler, place, year).
2. A short **"About this book"** preface: why you made it, what's included.
3. A **sources and privacy note**: your method (interviews, records),
   attribution practice, and a line about living-person privacy.
4. The chapters, in the order they tell the story.
5. Appendices: family tree overview, photo index, the source log's
   bibliography.
6. A page of thanks — name the people who told the stories.

That preface and privacy note matter: they tell every future reader exactly
how to trust (and extend) the book.

## Step 2 — Choose your toolchain (free options galore)

Because this guide is local-first, the following all work offline on your
computer:

- **Write in Markdown or Word.** Markdown keeps you tool-agnostic; word
  processors keep track changes. Either is fine; don't argue with yourself.
- **Build print-ready PDFs** with a free layout tool or word processor
  (export at 6×9 in or 8.5×11 in with real margins for print).
- **Optional book templates** from print-on-demand services give you
  margins and running heads for free.

You don't need a paid design suite. A clean font, generous margins, real
page numbers, and consistent caption styling outsell most decoration.

## Step 3 — Design choices that always sell family books

- **One serif font** for body text, one for captions. That's the whole
  font plan.
- **Photos near their stories**, not bundled in a distant photo section.
  A caption per image (your caption files have these ready).
- **Chapters short.** 4–10 pages each beats 30-page monsters; readers in
  busy decades finish short chapters.
- **Rich white space.** Big margins look like money in family books.
- **Page numbers and a simple table of contents.** Non-negotiable.

## Step 4 — Choose a print path (probably print-on-demand)

For a family book, you usually want **short-run printing**, not mass
distribution. Compare, per service, three things before you commit:

1. **Cost per copy** at your realistic quantity (probably 10–50 copies).
2. **Privacy:** do they need the *content* online, or just a PDF upload? Is
   the book catalogued publicly or (your choice) private — sold only to
   people you share the link with?
3. **Formatting demands:** page size, bleed, cover templates.

Practical notes:

- **Print-on-demand** (upload a PDF; they print copies as ordered) is
  ideal for family books; you keep the files and can order more copies
  whenever a cousin gets married.
- **A local print shop** printing 20 copies often beats POD pricing at
  small quantities — and keeps your manuscript entirely local. Great
  option for the privacy-conscious.
- **A saddle-stapled or spiral "workshop draft"** from an office printer is
  a legitimate *Step 0*: give drafts to relatives for comments before the
  "real" version exists. Their corrections are gold.

Whatever the path, **order one proof copy before you print the batch.**
Check margins, photo darkness, and caption placement in your hands. There is
always something.

## Step 5 — The rights check, briefly and honestly

For a private family book, this is usually simple: family interviews,
family photos, and records are yours to use, provided you got consent where
the [privacy chapter](04-privacy.html) asks for it. Two cautions:

- **Old photographs behind other families' walls:** if a distant family or
  society owns the photo, ask politely; whoever made/saved it may hold the
  original, not the copyright, but asking is free and usually works.
- **Quoting from published works** (a local history book, a song lyric):
  brief quotation with credit is standard practice; a full lyric or long
  excerpt is not. Keep quotations short and credited.

You are not a publisher of the public's material — you're a compiler of
yours. Keep it that way and rights stay boring, which is the ideal state.

## Step 6 — Distribution: the "family edition" and the "archive edition"

- **Family edition:** the copies that go to relatives, at cost or as gifts.
  Add a "FAMILY COPY — DO NOT REDISTRIBUTE" line inside the cover if it
  contains any consented living-person details (see
  [privacy](04-privacy.html)).
- **Archive edition:** one copy to each of: a genealogy society or family
  organization that preserves family books; a hometown library; your own
  fireproof box. Family books are lost constantly — archive copies are how
  the work outlives your hard drive times three.

Skip the public bookstore listings unless you genuinely want them; a family
book shines brightest as a *gift with a preface*, not as a product page.
If you do want a wider publication someday, revisit options then — the
files will be ready.

## Checklist before you press print

1. Every fact in the book checked against your source log (Chapter 3).
2. Living-person pass done and privacy log complete (Chapter 4).
3. Chapter voices sound like you, reread aloud (Chapter 5).
4. Photos credited with names, dates, places, and who identified them.
5. Front matter: preface, privacy note, contents, thanks.
6. Bibliography (your source log, cleaned) in the appendix.
7. One proof copy ordered and physically inspected.
8. Two archive copies planned for preservation.
9. All source files (scans, audio, transcripts, drafts) backed up in two places.

Then sleep well: a verified, private-respecting, warm, beautifully printed
family book is a thing very few families on earth have — and yours now does.
