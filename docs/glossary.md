# Glossary

*Every term used in this manual, defined like a friend would. Entries are
alphabetical; cross-references look like → see.*

## B

**Back up / backup (noun)** — a second copy of your files kept somewhere
other than the original device. This manual asks for backups in **two**
places (a second drive plus a folder at another location), because hard
drives fail quietly and houses flood.

**Bibliography** — a list of the sources you used, usually at the back of the
book. Yours can be plain: author, title, where seen, when seen. Your source
log's bibliography section is already most of one.

## C

**Census record** — an official count of a country's people, taken regularly
(commonly every ten years), listing household members, ages, jobs, and
birthplaces. In the U.S., detailed censuses are released to the public after
72 years, so today you can read censuses up to 1950. One of the best free
starting records for family research.

**Citation** — a note saying exactly where a fact came from, so another
person (or you, in ten years) can find it again. Plain citation format is
fine: *Maya Smith, interview audio 01 at 14:30.*

**Compilation** — a book or file built by gathering material from several
places. Your family book is a compilation. It should cite each source it
gathers from.

**Consent** — a person's clear yes to a specific use of their information or
story. In this manual, consent is verbal-okay-plus-written-down, and it can
be withdrawn at any time — then honored.

## D

**DPI (dots per inch)** — a scan's resolution. Higher DPI = more detail.
600 DPI is plenty for ordinary photos; 1200 for small photos you may enlarge.
Scanners let you set this per scan.

## H

**Hallucination** — the plain-English "AI confidently made that up" problem.
An AI states a guess in the same tone as a fact. The manual's answer is the
**verification routine**: nothing goes in the book until a real record shows it.

**Hallucinated citation** — a worst-case version: an AI invents a
source — title, page, quote — that doesn't exist. The cure is the same as
for hallucination: *open* the source it named. If you can't open it, it
doesn't count.

## L

**Living-person rule** — the manual's privacy backbone: details of anyone
alive (or possibly alive) stay private unless they consented — no addresses,
emails, phone numbers, full birth dates, medical or legal details, and
minimals for children. See → Privacy chapter.

**Local-first** — doing the work on your own computer, from your own files,
with no requirement to upload family material to any service. Manual's
default posture. Cloud backup stays optional and your choice.

## M

**Manifest (passenger list)** — a ship's official list of the people
traveling on it. Passenger manifests often include names, ages, last
residence, and destination — a favorite record for tracing immigration.

## O

**OCR (optical character recognition)** — software that converts pictures of
text into searchable text. Wonderful for finding words in scanned documents;
expect errors, and check anything important against the original image.

**Original record** — the document itself (or an exact image of it), as
distinguished from a transcription, index, or someone's summary of it.
Originals beat copies; copies beat summaries; summaries beat AI output.

## P

**POD (print on demand)** — a printing service that prints copies of a book
only as they're ordered, from a file you upload. Great fit for family books:
keep the file, order copies when a cousin marries.

**Preface** — the short opening section before Chapter 1, where you explain
why you made the book, how you verified facts, and how you protected living
relatives' privacy.

**Primary source** — a record or story made *at the time* by someone with
direct knowledge: a letter, a certificate, an interview with the person who
saw it. Preferred over secondhand memory and everything.

**Proof copy** — one physically printed copy you inspect before printing the
whole batch. There is always a margin to fix. Always order the proof.

## R

**Redaction** — removing or replacing sensitive details from a document
before sharing. In this manual, redact by *replacing* ("born in the 1970s")
rather than starring out, and log each change → see Privacy chapter.

## S

**Source log** — your running list of every claim, who proposed it, what
record proves it, where you saw that record, and whether it's verified. The
spine of the whole project → see your worksheet.

**Spine (of a chapter)** — the sentence that says what a chapter is really
about ("the bakery ran on gossip as much as bread"). Chapters gather rank:
anecdotes that support the spine; everything else waits.

## T

**Transcription** — the typed-out text of an audio recording (interviews) or
handwritten document (letters, album notes). AI transcription is fast but
not perfect: keep the audio, check quotes against it, and note when a
transcription was machine-made.

**Transcript** — the document produced by transcribing. Store transcripts in
the `transcripts/` folder with their audio.

## U

**UNVERIFIED** — the status a claim gets the moment someone (or an AI)
proposes it, before any record confirms it. Written in all caps in the
source log so it's impossible to overlook.
