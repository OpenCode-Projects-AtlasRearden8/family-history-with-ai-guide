# Scanning and Organizing Photos

*Photos are the artifact everyone will flip to first in the finished book.
Give them names, dates, and places — otherwise they become mysterious
portraits of unnamed strangers within two generations.*

## The two-pass method (do not scan everything at once)

Beginners try to digitize every photo in one heroic weekend and burn out.
Instead:

- **Pass 1 — Inventory:** photograph each album page or photo pile with your
  phone, quickly, and write down what you have ([worksheet](../worksheets/photo-inventory.html)).
  One shoebox ≈ 30 minutes.
- **Pass 2 — Select and scan properly:** scan only the photos that matter for
  stories and chapters, at good quality, one session at a time.

Pass 1 is cheap and protects you: if the album aunt refuses to loan it, you
still have reference images; and it tells you where the gold is.

## Taking phone photos of photos (Pass 1)

- Bright, indirect daylight. No flash (it glares).
- Fill the frame; hold the phone parallel, not angled.
- Include the whole page — writing on backs, captions, album notes matter.
- Photograph the album's *order too*. Photo order often encodes meaning.

## Scanning properly (Pass 2)

- Scanner settings: 600 DPI color for anything smaller than 8×10; 1200 DPI
  for tiny photos you might enlarge.
- Scan in color even for black and white; tone detail survives better.
- **Scan the backs.** Pencil notes from 1962 on a photo back are primary
  sources.
- Handle old photos by the edges; nitrile gloves optional, clean dry hands
  required.

No scanner? Pass 1 phone photos are often good enough to work from, and a
borrowed scanner hour at a relative's or library beats waiting to buy one.

## Naming: the convention that saves your future

Name files the moment they exist, in this pattern:

```
YYYY-MM_or_YYYYDD _surname-firstkeyword _number .jpg
1950_smithbakery_01.jpg
1938circa_kowalskiwedding_02.jpg
scan_uncertain-smith-boy_01.jpg
```

Rules:

- **Date first** (best approximation: `1950`, `1950circa`, `1950-06`).
- **Surname + a keyword** you'll actually search later.
- **No spaces or personal detail in filenames.**
- Match number to the source log line; the log holds the *why*.

Renaming 2,000 photos later is misery. Naming at scan time costs nothing.

## Folder structure that scales

```
family-history/
  originals/            # untouched, forever
    2026-10_scouting/   # scanning sessions, by month
  photos/
    by-surname/
      smith/
        1950_smithbakery_01.jpg
    unidentified/
  documents/
  recordings/
  transcripts/
  book/
    drafts/
    images/
```

`originals/` is sacred: scan once, copy freely, edit only copies. The
`unidentified/` folder is your to-do list, not a shame pile.

## Where AI helps (and where to stay skeptical)

Good AI tasks:

- **Suggesting filename keywords** from an image description.
- **Estimating a date** from visual clues (uniform style, car model,
  hairstyle) — but this is a *hypothesis to verify*, never a fact.
- **Transcribing handwriting** on backs and in albums — check its reading
  against the original at full zoom; handwriting AIs are confidently wrong.
- **Describing a damaged photo** to help you spot features (logos, signage)
  you can then identify for real.
- **Drafting a folder plan** from your inventory list.

AI does **not** reliably identify the *people* or *places* in photos. Some
tools will offer confident names for faces; unless your relative identified
the person (in person or in writing on the back), treat the AI's name as a
guess to verify. Mark anything unconfirmed as `unidentified` rather than
guessing a label. A wrong name attached to the right face is a family-history
poison pill: it spreads happily through every future copy of your archive.
Verify or mark unknown — that's the whole rule.

## Captions: the difference between a photo and a story

While memory is fresh, write a caption file (even one `.txt` per folder):

```
1950_smithbakery_01.jpg
Taken outside Smith's Bakery, Elm Street, fall 1950 (Aunt Maya's guess).
Left to right: Grandpa Joe, his brother Walt (per Maya 2026-10-11).
Maya laughed telling the story of the flour fight that led to this photo.
Sources: 2026-10-11 Smith interview, audio 01 at 14:30.
```

Names, place, date, who says so, and the story. That caption turns a picture
into history. When AI drafts book captions from your notes (it's good at
this), verify every name and date against this file before including it.

## Backups and prints

- Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies, two different devices, one somewhere
  else. Local-first means two external/back-up drives can cover it; a cloud
  backup is optional, your call.
- Once a year, print a small album of favorites. Plenty of family history
  was lost to a single failed hard drive; paper survives house moves and
  format changes.
- Keep originals of any physical photo you're given; digitize your copy and
  return the original with thanks.
